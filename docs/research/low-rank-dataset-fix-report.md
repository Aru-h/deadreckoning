# Redundant sensors and noise-aware thresholds restore rank

The UAV dataset is numerically full rank because every one of the 9 channels carries independent continuous noise, and noise of any size above roughly 1e-12 lifts a rank-deficient matrix to rank min(m,n)=9 with probability 1; numpy's default tolerance and Person B's 1e-10 only detect roundoff-level dependence, not noise ([numpy docs](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.linalg.matrix_rank.html)). Behind the noise, the clean matrix has rank 3, because every clean channel lies in span{1, t, t^2}, but on the team's file the third clean singular value is only about **0.054** against a noise floor near 0.1, so even a noise-aware rule finds effective rank 2. Projecting onto an all-channel basis is the identity (GPS unchanged, RMSE 0.099), and projecting onto an IMU-only basis fails (RMSE 3.97 in the team's run, reproduced here) because constant accelerations and noise do not contain the position. **Recommended fix:** regenerate the data with redundant, linearly related channels (3 GPS receivers per axis, barometer, velocity, acceleration, gyro) and stronger curvature (a2 about 0.15/0.10/0.08), so the clean matrix is rank 3 with a visible gap. Then change one step in Person B's code: a noise-aware Gavish-Donoho threshold on sigma-whitened columns, with Q taken as the top-r left singular vectors instead of a greedy Gram-Schmidt over raw columns. In 200-seed simulation this gives rank 3 in 200/200 seeds, GPS RMSE 0.099 -> 0.046 after projection -> 0.0154 after projection plus least squares, and 0.0093 with receiver averaging. Real public datasets (INSANE, Zurich MAV, ALFA) do not fix the rank problem, since real noise also gives full rank; they are suitable only as a separate effective-rank demo. All RMSE and singular-value figures below were computed in simulation (numpy), not taken from literature.

## Noise makes any matrix full rank, and the 1e-10 tolerance cannot see it

Rank-deficient matrices are a measure-zero set, so adding continuous i.i.d. noise to a rank-r matrix gives full rank almost surely. This is standard genericity reasoning (Trefethen & Bau, Golub & Van Loan), which the research did not fetch, so treat it as textbook-level rather than a cited theorem. numpy's default tolerance is `S.max() * max(M,N) * eps`, and it is designed to separate true zeros from SVD roundoff ([numpy docs](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.linalg.matrix_rank.html)); for a 100 x 9 matrix that is about 1e-12 times the largest singular value. Weyl's inequality says each singular value moves by at most the spectral norm of the noise, so the 6 "empty" singular values sit near sigma*sqrt(m), not near zero.

A toy check in simulation (m=100, n=9, true rank 3, sigma=0.05) shows the pattern: noisy singular values 46.2, 21.5, 16.9, then 0.575, 0.529, 0.504, ... 0.364, with `matrix_rank` returning 9 for both the default and tol=1e-10, while the clean matrix returns 3. The team's real file (101 x 9) behaves the same way: singular values 117.56, 17.57, 1.153, 0.166, 0.154, ... 0.089, `matrix_rank` = 9, Person B's `calculate_rank` = 9, and `find_independent_columns` keeps 9 of 9 columns. So the rank-9 result is expected behaviour and not a bug in Person B's code. The same fact explains the two projection failures. Projecting GPS onto the span of all 9 columns is the identity on GPS, leaving RMSE at 0.0926/0.1176/0.1243 (x/y/z). Projecting onto the 6 IMU columns gives RMSE 3.97/2.43/1.20 in simulation on the real file, matching the team's 3.97. The tolerance is not the cause of the second failure: even the top-1 or top-3 IMU subspaces give about 4.0/2.45/1.20, because acceleration and gyro channels carry no position information beyond the constant and linear terms. In the redesigned dataset an IMU-only basis spans only {1, t}, since velocity is linear in t and acceleration is constant, so position needs t^2 and the IMU-only Q fails (RMSE 0.89 overall) even with redundancy. Q must therefore include at least one position-type channel.

## Effective rank counts singular values above the noise floor

Define the effective rank as the number of singular values above a threshold tied to the noise level, not to machine epsilon. Three tools set that threshold. The Marchenko-Pastur law puts the largest noise singular value near sigma(sqrt(m)+sqrt(n)) for an m x n matrix of i.i.d. noise with std sigma ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchenko%E2%80%93Pastur_distribution)); for m=100, n=9, sigma=0.05 that is 0.65 against observed noise values of 0.364-0.575. The Gavish-Donoho hard threshold is the MSE-optimal cutoff for singular-value truncation: for a square n x n matrix with known sigma it is tau* = (4/sqrt(3)) sqrt(n) sigma, and for m x n with beta=m/n it is lambda*(beta) sqrt(n) sigma with lambda*(beta)=sqrt(2(beta+1)+8beta/((beta+1)+sqrt(beta^2+14beta+1))); with unknown sigma it is omega(beta) times the median singular value ([Gavish & Donoho, arXiv:1305.5870](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.5870)). In the toy example these give 0.784 (known sigma) and 0.839 (median rule), both above the largest noise value, while the bare MP edge of 0.65 sits uncomfortably close to the top noise value 0.575. Finally, a visible gap between s_r and s_{r+1} is the informal check; it works only with clear SNR.

Three caveats matter for this project, all from the notes. First, the formulas assume i.i.d. zero-mean noise with equal variance in every channel, which this dataset violates (GPS 0.1, acc 0.015, gyro 0.01), and the notes confirm that GD with unknown sigma on the un-whitened matrix fails here (reports rank 4 on the redesigned matrix, correct only 1% of seeds). Dividing each column by its own sigma first is necessary. Second, the n in sqrt(n) is the larger dimension (101 timestamps); using the 9 channels would give a threshold of 0.235, below the noise. Third, the asymptotic theory is for large matrices, and 101 x 9 or 101 x 19 is small, so the rules are guides, and the GD threshold is MSE-optimal rather than a pure detector. If the weakest signal singular value is below the noise edge, rank is not recoverable in principle. That is exactly the situation in the original data, as the next section shows.

## Redundant channels plus stronger curvature give a detectable rank 3

The signal structure is simple: a quadratic trajectory a + bt + ct^2 sampled at n times is a vector in col(V) with V=[1 t t^2]; position, velocity (b+2ct) and acceleration (2c) are all linear in (a,b,c), so any set of such channels stacked as columns has clean rank at most 3 ([Boyd, EE263 Lecture 7](https://see.stanford.edu/materials/lsoeldsee263/transcripts/IntroToLinearDynamicalSystems-Lecture07.html), a pointer only; the stacked "rank-3" framing is the notes' own synthesis, and no tutorial stating it was found). The notes tested three candidate fixes.

| Candidate | What changes | Result (200 seeds, computed) | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Redundant channels + stronger curvature (recommended) | Regenerate data; B uses whitened GD rank and top-r SVD basis | rank 3 found 200/200; projection 0.0464; +LS 0.0154; fused 3 receivers +LS 0.0093 | Needs new CSV; extra channels; one change to B |
| 2. Vandermonde basis [1,t,t^2] as Q | Basis from the model, B's code unchanged | Projection 0.0154, identical to LS on raw data (coefficient difference 3e-14) | Projection and LS become the same operation, so C's two stages are redundant; the model is injected into the basis |
| 3. Keep data, change only the threshold | No data change | Effective rank 2; projection 0.079; +LS 0.0154; r=1 destroys signal (0.76/0.55/1.49) | Zero effort, but no clean 3-vs-6 low-rank story; IMU-only Q still fails |

For candidate 1 the notes used 3 GPS receivers per axis (gps_x, gps2_x, gps3_x, ...), a barometer for z, velocity channels, constant acceleration, level-flight gyro_x/gyro_y as pure noise and gyro_z as a prescribed yaw rate, giving a 101 x 19 matrix (101 x 28 with 6 receivers). The original curvature (a2 = 0.05/0.03/0.01) is too weak: the third clean singular value is 0.34 against a whitened noise bulk near 1, and Marchenko-Pastur detects rank 3 in only about 50% of seeds, while GD with known sigma always reports 2. Raising a2 to (0.15, 0.10, 0.08) makes the clean singular values 323.8, 34.97, 2.263, then exactly 0 to machine precision. After whitening by per-column sigma the noisy values are 3198, 354.8, 42.95, then 13.6 and below, against an MP edge of 14.41 and a GD threshold of 17.0, so rank 3 is clear. Detection over 200 seeds is MP 0.99 and GD 1.00 for 3 receivers (MP 0.98, GD 1.00 for 6). Tripling the original curvature already gave 100% correct detection under GD; the notes did not search for the minimal sufficient value. An a2 of (0.15, 0.10, 0.08) corresponds to accelerations of 0.30, 0.20 and 0.16 m/s^2, a gentle but plausible UAV flight.

Honest accounting of the gains matters for the viva. Projection alone gives roughly a 2-2.7x error reduction (0.099 -> 0.046 with 3 receivers, 0.036 with 6). Projection plus least squares (0.0154) is no better than least squares on a single raw GPS channel (0.0154), and for extrapolation to t in (10, 12] projection adds nothing (0.0342 vs 0.0345). The real benefit of redundancy is fusion: averaging 3 receivers then least squares gives 0.0093, and 6 receivers 0.0065. A leave-one-out check (evaluated channel excluded from the SVD) gives 0.0573 unscaled, so the improvement is not self-leakage; the excess over the oracle 0.0154 is subspace-estimation error. Suggested viva message: projection removes the part of each noisy channel that is inconsistent with the common motion subspace, and least squares then imposes the physical model. A limit of these results is that noise is independent white Gaussian; real receivers share multipath and ionospheric errors, and outliers, bias and correlated noise were not tested.

## Minimal changes to Person B and the generator

Person B's existing functions fail on the new noisy data (19 of 19 columns kept, Q identity-like). Because greedy Gram-Schmidt over raw noisy columns projects a held-out receiver onto its own noisy neighbours (RMSE 0.0997, no gain), and a noise-aware tolerance applied only to the greedy column picker is also poor (0.091), the tolerance alone is not the fix; the basis must be the top-r singular subspace. The change validated in simulation is:

```python
import numpy as np
def gd_threshold(n, p):                      # Gavish-Donoho hard threshold, known sigma = 1
    b = p / n
    return np.sqrt(2*(b+1) + 8*b/((b+1) + np.sqrt(b*b + 14*b + 1))) * np.sqrt(n)

def noise_aware_rank(M, col_sigma):          # replaces calculate_rank(M, 1e-10) on noisy data
    s = np.linalg.svd(M / col_sigma, compute_uv=False)
    return int(np.sum(s > gd_threshold(*M.shape)))

def signal_basis(M, col_sigma):              # replaces find_independent_columns + gram_schmidt
    r = noise_aware_rank(M, col_sigma)
    U = np.linalg.svd(M / col_sigma, full_matrices=False)[0][:, :r]
    return gram_schmidt(U), r                # U already orthonormal; GS kept for the exercise
```

Here `col_sigma` comes from the datasheet noise levels documented in the CSV header or README (so the threshold is defensible as "datasheet noise level"), or from the residual std of a per-column quadratic least-squares fit; estimating it that way found rank 3 in 200/200 seeds with projection 0.0460 and +LS 0.01537, versus 0.0464 / 0.01538 with known sigma. Using top singular vectors also avoids Gram-Schmidt's weakness on nearly dependent columns. Moler reports that classical Gram-Schmidt loses orthogonality roughly like the square of the condition number, modified Gram-Schmidt like the first power, and Householder QR stays near machine precision ([Moler, MathWorks blog](https://blogs.mathworks.com/cleve/2016/10/27/apologies-to-gram-schmidt/)). The exponent for the modified variant is **unresolved in the sources read**: lecture notes on Björck's analysis, as summarised by the fetch tool, show kappa^2 in the MGS bound ([UPenn CIS610](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~cis610/Gram-Schmidt-Bjorck.pdf)), which conflicts with Moler, and the notes' own Läuchli test (MGS error 1.2e-8 at eps=1e-8, classical 0.707) is consistent with the first power; the same UPenn notes report classical orthogonality degrading to about 0.54 on an ill-conditioned 50 x 10 example while MGS stays near 4.6e-8. Treat the exponent as unverified and check Björck (1967), Golub & Van Loan section 5.2.8 or Higham before quoting it. If the assignment requires Gram-Schmidt, apply it to U_k (already orthonormal to about 1.6e-15) and report ||I - Q^T Q||. The team's rank choice is also a decision B must document, since the 'quadratic' structure is encoded in the data, not discovered.

The draft generator (tested; the sample file written with seed 42 has 101 rows and 23 columns, and keeps the original column names plus gps2_*/gps3_*, baro_z, vel_*):

```python
import numpy as np, csv
COEF = {'x': (2, .8, .15), 'y': (1, .5, .10), 'z': (5, .2, .08)}   # a0, a1, a2

def generate(seed=0, n=101, dt=0.1, sig_gps=0.10, sig_vel=0.05, sig_acc=0.015, sig_gyro=0.01,
             sig_baro=0.20, n_rx=3, yaw=(0.0, 0.05, 0.01), coef=COEF, write=None):
    """Every clean channel lies in span{1,t,t^2} -> clean rank 3; noisy matrix is full rank."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed); t = np.arange(n) * dt
    P = {a: c[0] + c[1]*t + c[2]*t**2 for a, c in coef.items()}     # position
    V = {a: c[1] + 2*c[2]*t for a, c in coef.items()}               # velocity
    A = {a: 2*c[2] + 0*t for a, c in coef.items()}                  # acceleration
    cols = {'time': t}
    for a in 'xyz':
        cols[f'gps_{a}'] = P[a] + rng.normal(0, sig_gps, n)
        for k in range(2, n_rx + 1):
            cols[f'gps{k}_{a}'] = P[a] + rng.normal(0, sig_gps, n)
    cols['baro_z'] = P['z'] + rng.normal(0, sig_baro, n)
    for a in 'xyz':
        cols[f'vel_{a}'] = V[a] + rng.normal(0, sig_vel, n)
        cols[f'acc_{a}'] = A[a] + rng.normal(0, sig_acc, n)
    cols['gyro_x'] = rng.normal(0, sig_gyro, n)                     # level flight
    cols['gyro_y'] = rng.normal(0, sig_gyro, n)
    cols['gyro_z'] = yaw[1] + 2*yaw[2]*t + rng.normal(0, sig_gyro, n)  # yaw psi(t)=p0+p1 t+p2 t^2
    for a in 'xyz': cols[f'true_{a}'] = P[a]
    if write:
        with open(write, 'w', newline='') as f:
            w = csv.writer(f); w.writerow(cols.keys())
            for i in range(n): w.writerow([cols[k][i] for k in cols])
    return t, cols
```

Noise levels were re-estimated from the original CSV (GPS std 0.092/0.118/0.124, acc 0.015, gyro 0.010), so sigma_gps=0.10, sig_acc=0.015 and sig_gyro=0.01 match the team's data. Two physical-consistency points: gyro_x/gyro_y as pure noise is acceptable only for level, non-rolling flight, and the original gyro_z as pure noise is unphysical on a curved, accelerating path. The prescribed yaw polynomial keeps gyro_z inside span{1, t}, so the rank argument is untouched; a more realistic heading-follows-velocity gyro_z lies 3.6% (original coefficients) to 19.8% (new coefficients) outside the span but at 0.0005-0.011 rad/s, below one gyro sigma, so rank detection is practically unaffected. Person A's 3x3 calibration system is untouched in design, but its interaction with the new channels was not examined.

## Effects on Persons A, C and D, and what real datasets can and cannot do

Person A's data and RREF/LU work stay as they are, since any 3 channels still work, though the calibration interplay with the new columns is untested. Person C's projection code and quadratic least squares run unchanged: the quadratic fit recovers the true coefficients and the residual norm is about sigma*sqrt(n-3), so least squares remains meaningful. Person C should report projection and least squares as two stages with honest numbers, and note that on one channel they match (0.0154 each), which is the penalty of candidate 2 made visible. Person D's PCA also needs no code change, but a choice: use the covariance of the sigma-whitened channels, not the correlation matrix. On the new data the centered covariance of the whitened channels has 2 dominant eigenvalues (23427, 18.83) over a noise bulk mean of 0.92, not 3, because centering removes the constant direction and signal rank is 3 minus 1. Raw-unit eigenvalues are 236.7, 0.0911, 0.0294, ... so the second is already faint; the plain correlation matrix gives 14.02, 1.27, 1.20, 1.07, and the second signal eigenvalue is lost in the bulk. On the original file the second centered eigenvalue (0.0156) is indistinguishable from GPS noise variance (0.0133), so the original data supports only 1 dominant component, which is another reason to regenerate. D's code was not available, so these statements assume `np.cov` on the channel matrix.

Public datasets do not fix the core issue, because real noise makes a 100 x 9 crop numerically full rank just as simulated noise does. What they can offer is a real-data effective-rank demo alongside the synthetic main dataset. Of the sources checked, INSANE is the best candidate: 18 sensors including two 8 Hz RTK GNSS receivers and IMUs at 200-900 Hz, RTK ground truth with 1-16 cm outdoor accuracy, and CSV files with headers ([INSANE paper, arXiv 2210.09114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.09114)); its host page (https://sst.aau.at/cns/datasets/insane-dataset/) could not be read, so size, licence, login and column names are unverified. Its outdoor ground truth and GNSS may both be RTK-derived, so the noisy-versus-truth contrast may be small (unverified). The Zurich Urban MAV dataset has GPS, accelerometer, gyro and barometer in CSV with no usage restriction, but its metric ground truth is photogrammetric camera positions at image times, sparse and a poor match for RMSE-versus-time comparisons, and its column names and rates are not documented on the page ([Zurich MAV](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/zurichmavdataset.html)). ALFA offers processed CSV with GPS and IMU topics from 47 fixed-wing flights, but its labels are fault times and types, not position ground truth, and its download page returned 403 ([ALFA, arXiv 1907.06268](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06268)). EuRoC and UZH-FPV have accurate ground truth but no GPS ([UZH-FPV](https://fpv.ifi.uzh.ch/)); the IEEE DataPort drone sensor-fusion set needs a subscription ([IEEE DataPort](https://www.ieee-dataport.org/documents/drone-sensor-fusion-dataset)). Reuse would add preprocessing work that the new generator avoids: convert geodetic to local ENU, put everything on one timeline (average IMU samples in each GPS interval; do not upsample GPS), check units and frames, crop about 100 moving rows, and standardise columns before PCA. These steps are the notes' own recommendations, not sourced. The expected effective rank of a real crop (perhaps 2-4) is an estimate; no real spectrum was computed. A real crop would also have no known sigma per channel, so the whitened GD rule would need estimated sigmas, and real accelerometers are dominated by noise and gravity, adding rank rather than removing it.

## Conclusion

The rank problem is not a bug in the team's code but a mismatch between what the data contains and what the steps ask of it: the original design (three GPS signals, constant accelerations, zero gyro, third clean singular value 0.054) has too little redundancy and too little curvature to give a 3-versus-6 story under noise. Redundant channels, stronger curvature and a whitened Gavish-Donoho rank with a singular-vector basis restore a meaningful pipeline at the cost of one B function and a regenerated CSV. A less obvious lesson is how modest the benefit is: projection alone gives about 2x, projection plus least squares ties least squares alone, and fusing receivers is what actually drops the error to 0.0093; presenting those numbers honestly is stronger in a viva than claiming projection does the denoising by itself. Before committing, the team should rerun the generator with their own seeds, open INSANE or Zurich headers directly if they want a real-data demo, and settle the Gram-Schmidt exponent from Björck or Higham before stating it.
